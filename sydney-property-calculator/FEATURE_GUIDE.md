## 🎓 Understanding the Analysis

### What is "Recovery Time"?
Recovery time is when your property's increase in value equals the stamp duty you paid.

**Example:**
- Property price: $800,000
- Stamp duty paid: $31,930
- Property needs to reach: $831,930 to "recover" the stamp duty cost

### Why Does It Matter?

1. **Investment Perspective** - Helps you understand the true cost of buying
2. **Market Risk** - Shows how different growth scenarios affect your investment
3. **Timing Decisions** - Helps evaluate when/where to buy
4. **Opportunity Cost** - That stamp duty money could be invested elsewhere

### What Affects Recovery?

✅ **Property Growth Rate** - Higher growth = faster recovery
❌ **Interest Rates** - Don't directly affect recovery time (but affect loan costs)
✅ **Stamp Duty Amount** - Less stamp duty = faster recovery
✅ **First Home Buyer Status** - Concessions reduce stamp duty, faster recovery

### Important Disclaimers

⚠️ **Not Included in Analysis:**
- Selling costs (agent fees 2-3%, marketing ~$5k-15k, legal fees)
- Opportunity cost of capital
- Transaction costs
- Market volatility (assumes steady growth)
- Maintenance, rates, insurance
- Tax implications

⚠️ **Assumptions:**
- Consistent annual growth rate
- No additional costs or income
- Linear appreciation (reality is more volatile)

## 💡 Practical Examples

### Scenario 1: Strong Market (+7% growth)
- **Recovery Time:** 2.3 years
- **Interpretation:** Your property value covers stamp duty quickly
- **Strategy:** Good time to buy if you believe in strong growth

### Scenario 2: Moderate Market (+5% growth)
- **Recovery Time:** 3.2 years
- **Interpretation:** Reasonable recovery period
- **Strategy:** Average market conditions, typical holding period works

### Scenario 3: Weak Market (+1% growth)
- **Recovery Time:** 15.9 years
- **Interpretation:** Very slow recovery
- **Strategy:** Consider waiting or focus on other benefits (housing, lifestyle)

### Scenario 4: Negative Market (-2% or -5% growth)
- **Recovery Time:** Never recovers
- **Interpretation:** Property value decreases, stamp duty becomes a sunk cost
- **Strategy:** High risk scenario, only buy if you need the property regardless

## 🔮 Using Interest Rate Scenarios

While interest rates don't directly affect recovery time, they provide important context:

### Unchanged (5.34%)
- Current market conditions continue
- Predictable loan costs

### Cut to 3.5%
- Lower interest rates
- More affordable repayments
- Often correlates with economic slowdown (may affect property growth)

### Cut then Bounce
- Initial relief, then normalization
- Variable costs over time
- Need to budget for rate increases

### Raise to 7%
- Higher interest rates
- Higher repayments
- May correlate with inflation (property prices may grow faster)

## 🎯 Making Decisions

Use this tool to:

1. **Set Realistic Expectations** - Understand what growth you need
2. **Evaluate Risk** - See downside scenarios, not just optimistic ones
3. **Compare Markets** - Different areas have different growth rates
4. **Time Your Purchase** - Is now the right time, or should you wait?
5. **Understand Trade-offs** - Balance stamp duty against other factors

## ✅ Key Takeaways

- **Recovery ≠ Profit** - Just means you've covered the stamp duty cost
- **Multiple Scenarios** - Always consider best, average, and worst cases
- **Context Matters** - Interest rates, growth rates, personal circumstances
- **Long-term View** - Property is typically a long-term investment
- **Professional Advice** - Use this as a guide, consult financial advisors
